Secure Photo Blur

Privacy-first photo anonymization — offline, open-source, no personal data collected.

Secure Photo Blur is a free, open-source web app that blurs faces and sensitive areas in your photos before you share them publicly. Automatic face detection finds faces for you; you can adjust, add or remove regions manually, then export an anonymized copy. Open the app →

How it works

The whole tool is a static web page with no backend — your photos never leave your device. It runs fully offline after the first load (PWA): you can switch to airplane mode and it still works, or open DevTools → Network and confirm that nothing is ever uploaded. There is no account, no cookies and no tracking pixels — only anonymous, cookie-less page-view analytics via Cloudflare Web Analytics. A strict Content Security Policy blocks all external connections, the referrer is suppressed, and camera, microphone and geolocation permissions are explicitly disabled. Face detection runs locally via BlazeFace (the @vladmandic/human library), GPU-accelerated through WebGL, with model weights bundled and no external requests at runtime.

Blur methods and their security

MethodSecurityHow it works
MosaicHighAdaptive pixelation reduces each face to about 5×5 effective pixels (minimum block 12px), destroying spatial detail. Hard to reverse.
Solid blackMaximumSets every pixel to R=G=B=0 — 100% information destruction, provably irreversible. The Fantômas study (PoPETs 2024) found solid fill resisted all ML-based deanonymization attacks.
Solid average colorMaximumFills the region with its single average color — no structural detail, texture or shape survives.
Gaussian blurLowSoft blur only. Research shows AI can reverse it — Revelio (arXiv:2506.12344, June 2025) and Fantômas (PoPETs 2024). Use for non-sensitive content only.

What the research says

“It has a re-identification accuracy of 95.9% … The result suggests that Gaussian blur should not be used for face anonymization purposes.” — Revelio: Restoring Gaussian Blurred Face Images for Deanonymization Attacks (arXiv:2506.12344, 2025)
“11 out of 15 tested face anonymizations are at least partially reversible.” — Fantômas: Understanding Face Anonymization Reversibility (PoPETs 2024, arXiv:2210.10651)

Supported formats & languages

Imports JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIC/HEIF; all EXIF/GPS metadata is stripped automatically on export. Available in six languages — English, Italian, German, French, Spanish and Polish — auto-detected from your browser.

Frequently asked questions

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Everything runs locally in your browser — there is no backend and no uploads. After the first load the app works fully offline (PWA); you can switch to airplane mode and it still works.

Which blur method is safe against AI reversal?

Solid black and solid average-color destroy 100% of the pixel information and are provably irreversible. Mosaic reduces a face to about 5×5 effective pixels and is strong. Gaussian blur is NOT safe: research (Revelio, arXiv:2506.12344, 2025) shows AI can reverse it.

Is location and EXIF metadata removed?

Yes. Canvas re-rendering strips all EXIF/GPS metadata automatically on export. Your original file stays untouched and a new anonymized copy is created.

Is it free and open source?

Yes. It is free to use under the PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0 license and the source code is on GitHub and fully auditable — around 2,000 lines of Preact and TypeScript.

Which image formats are supported?

JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIC/HEIF. All EXIF/GPS metadata is stripped automatically on export.
